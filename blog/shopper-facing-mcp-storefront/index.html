Your catalog now has an address inside AI assistants

1 October 2026Docs
A storefront built from published dataThe merchant controls what the assistant understandsAn address for every published experienceA report for the channel

A Crossword brand's store can now answer inside ChatGPT and Claude. The brand publishes a shopper-facing MCP storefront, and those assistants call a per-workspace address when a shopper wants to search the real catalog or get help.

A merchant creates a separate AI assistant channel in Crossword and publishes it. ChatGPT and Claude use that address to search products, apply filters and return answers from the brand's help content. The shopper stays in the assistant. Checkout and payment stay on the merchant's website.

A storefront built from published data

Setup is where the merchant decides what the assistant can see. They choose the tables and fields the assistant may use, build the product card and add a shopping guide. Nothing is sent until those fields are selected. Publish locks the tools, fields, help sources, card setup and instructions. The address answers only with that published setup.

The card itself is the brand's. The merchant sets the store name, logo, currency, button text, button color and the product facts shown to the shopper. Results can appear as several product rows in one frame, with one "Shop on " button. That button takes the shopper to the merchant's website.

When Crossword shows those products as cards, ChatGPT and Claude are told the cards are already visible. They do not also receive the photos or product links in their text result, so a second product list does not appear under the cards.

The shopping guide tells the assistants what to learn before they search. A brand might ask who the product is for, or which size the shopper usually wears. Guided questions can offer short answers drawn from current catalog values, then wait for the shopper before the search runs.

The tools are product search, product filters, help search and product details. Product details can open one product or compare up to 10, including size, color, photo and per-size price and stock. A merchant can also add brand-defined shopping tools, built from staff-written saved queries. Those tools start switched off. They reach the assistants only after they are enabled and published.

The merchant controls what the assistant understands

Crossword lets merchants title each tool and describe what each input means. Shared columns can use a ready-made meaning, or an AI-written draft that staff review and accept with "Use." Hidden tool names stay generic once published. The titles the assistants see are the ones the brand wrote.

The Setup preview shows exactly what ChatGPT and Claude read. If details are missing, Publish lists the gaps and still lets the merchant continue.

Catalog values stay current after each sync, which matters for filters such as size, category or material. The main agent also covers tools from its helper agents, so the channel can expose the published catalog without the shopper needing to know how the merchant organized its agents.

An address for every published experience

Each channel has one MCP address, and Setup includes a button to regenerate it. Merchants can choose a Crossword address in the form label.mcp.crswrd.ai. Crossword handles the address, so the brand does not need to make a DNS change. Labels are first come, first served, and an address becomes live in about a minute. The earlier shop.crswrd.ai/<code> address continues to work and serves the same tools and cards.

The AI assistant channel is separate from the merchant's other channels and starts off. Once it is configured, published and switched on, the address answers with the current published setup. Switching access off makes the address stop answering within a minute, while the last published setup is kept for later use.

A report for the channel

The Report page shows tool calls, errors, response time, recent activity and searches that returned no results. Product links can carry tracking tags, and the channel keeps anonymous records of search and view events. The same report covers tagged website visits, add-to-carts and purchases that came through those AI assistant links.

Those website outcomes follow the boundary of this release. Discovery happens in ChatGPT or Claude. The shopper sees brand-styled cards, answers the brand's guided questions and opens the merchant's site through the shopping button. There is no shopper login in this channel, and the tools are read-only. Identity, cart and checkout remain on the merchant's website.
