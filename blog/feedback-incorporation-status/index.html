See when feedback has been reviewed, planned or incorporated

19 October 2026Docs
A status for every reportThe detail panel and the activity timelineAsking before the reporter is emailed

Merchants can now see what has happened to submitted feedback, and the team can move each report through a status. The reports themselves come from the widget and from people using the extension. How those reports are captured is in the extension-feedback post. This post is about the Widget feedback page in the dashboard, where the team reviews them and updates their status.

The page has a table of reports, with a status column and a filter, and a detail panel for a single report. Team members can show or hide columns, open a report, change its status and leave a comment on the activity timeline. The same page also has a board view, with a column for each status, for teams that prefer to move reports across columns.

A status for every report

Each report has a status the team can update. The shipped statuses are New, Under review, Planned, In progress and Incorporated. Filters can show one status or several at a time, and can also narrow by channel, by a search over email or note, and by a date range.

When a report arrives, and when its status later changes, the reporter can receive an email at the address they typed. Those settings apply to every channel in the workspace. A receipt email can go out as soon as the reporter hits Send. The default is to send that receipt. The default wording for each status is:

  • New: "We received your feedback about {site}. Thanks for letting us know."
  • Under review: "Our team is reviewing the issue you reported on {site}."
  • Planned: "We plan to fix the issue you reported on {site} in an upcoming update."
  • In progress: "We're working on a fix for the issue you reported on {site}."
  • Incorporated: "The issue you reported on {site} has been fixed. Thanks for letting us know."

{site} is the host of the reported page. A workspace can edit that wording, choose which status changes send an email and choose whether a receipt email goes out when a report arrives. Team comments on the timeline are only visible to the team.

The detail panel and the activity timeline

Opening a report opens a side panel. The panel holds the description, the images, links into the conversation and the run trace, the runs in that conversation, environment details and a debug timeline of console errors, connection events and widget events. It also holds the status control.

The Activity timeline records when the report was sent, status changes, team comments and whether a reporter email was sent or failed. A team member can add a comment for the team from the same panel.

A team member can update the status from the table, from the board or from the detail panel. Saving a status change writes a timeline event and updates the report immediately.

Asking before the reporter is emailed

By default, a status change that is set to email the reporter asks first. The control is a box labelled "Email the reporter about this change," ticked by default. In that ask mode, the change waits for Save. A workspace can switch the mode to automatic, so the email goes out as soon as the status changes, with no box.

If the email fails, the failure is recorded on the timeline, with a reason such as email not being set up, the service being unavailable, the address being rejected or the daily limit being reached. The status itself does not change back. A failed email does not undo the move.

Settings also let a workspace name team addresses that should be alerted when a new report arrives, up to ten addresses. Those alerts are separate from the emails the reporter receives.

The table columns a team can show or hide include email, status, description, images, conversation, last run, run status, channel, page URL, widget version and the created date. Image and thumbnail links are short-lived signed addresses, lasting five minutes per request. From the panel, a teammate can also copy the debug JSON, open the conversation and open the run trace. A board view uses the same statuses. This is where submitted reports are reviewed, planned and, when the work is done, marked incorporated, with a record of who moved them and whether the reporter was told.
