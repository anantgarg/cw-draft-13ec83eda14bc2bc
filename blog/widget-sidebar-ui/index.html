Crossword now opens as a storefront sidebar

15 September 2026Released
This post has no section headings.

We built a second way for shoppers to open Crossword on a storefront: a full-height sidebar that arrives from either edge of the page.

Crossword is an AI shopping assistant for ecommerce. The embedded widget has traditionally opened as a floating chat window. That format works well when the conversation should stay compact. Some shopping journeys need a steadier workspace, especially when a shopper wants to keep the assistant open while looking around the rest of the page.

The sidebar gives that conversation a fixed place. On desktop it opens as a 400px column against the left or right wall, with square corners and the full height of the viewport. The storefront behind it stays interactive. Clicking elsewhere on the page does not dismiss the conversation.

This sounds like a small layout choice. It changed how we had to think about the widget boundary, motion and configuration.

One channel, two ways to open

Merchants and our forward deployed engineers can choose the presentation per channel in the channel Style settings. The existing floating chat window remains the default for existing channels. A channel can be configured to open as a sidebar and the panel can be assigned to the left or right wall.

We kept panel side separate from launcher placement. The launcher controls where the shopper starts. The sidebar side controls where the conversation appears. A launcher can rest at the bottom right while the panel enters from the left.

That separation matters in real storefront layouts. Merchants already have navigation, carts, promotions and other controls competing for the edges of a page. Independent controls let the entry point fit one part of the layout and the open conversation fit another.

The same channel keeps the floating chat window option. Choosing a sidebar is an explicit channel decision, so an existing configuration keeps its current behavior until someone changes and publishes it.

A panel that behaves like part of the storefront

The desktop sidebar slides in from its configured wall. Its transcript moves with the panel, followed by the header and composer. Closing sends the panel back toward the same edge. The sequence makes the conversation feel attached to the wall instead of collapsing toward the launcher.

The page around the sidebar remains clickable during the open and closing states. That detail is essential. A transparent widget frame can easily capture clicks across an area that looks empty, especially when the launcher and panel sit on opposite sides. We constrained the interactive region so the visible widget does not turn the rest of the storefront into a dead zone.

We also aligned the responsive boundary with the existing phone experience. At 640px and below, the desktop column stands down and the widget uses the phone sheet. A 400px sidebar would leave little room for a storefront on those screens. The phone layout already handles that space.

Reduced motion is handled directly. When a visitor has that preference enabled, the open panel appears immediately and remains visible. They get the same usable sidebar without waiting through the entrance.

The launcher and the panel have different jobs

The work also made the closed state more configurable. A channel can use a circle launcher and can show suggestion chips beside it. Those suggestions can lead into the greeting when the shopper opens the conversation.

The launcher can also be hidden. In that state the closed widget is invisible and does not intercept page clicks. A merchant can still open it through the widget's programmatic open method, which supports storefront controls that start the assistant from elsewhere on the page.

These choices all preserve the same principle: opening the assistant should fit the storefront. The shopper may enter through a launcher, a suggestion or a merchant controlled action. Once open, the conversation can occupy a stable edge without blocking interaction with the visible page.

Keeping an open conversation in place

Channels can also remember whether the widget was open. When that setting is enabled, Crossword stores the open state and restores it as the shopper moves across pages. The cached appearance and remembered state are applied before the first visible render, so an open conversation can return already in place after a refresh without flashing closed first.

This is particularly useful for the sidebar. A shopper can keep the assistant available while navigating the site and continue with the storefront still usable beside it. The result is a channel-level presentation choice that carries through the whole visit, from the launcher to the wall and across page loads.
