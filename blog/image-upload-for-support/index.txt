15:T1698,<p>We added image upload to the human handover process in Crossword.</p>
<p>Crossword is an AI shopping assistant for ecommerce. A shopper can ask questions in the shopping conversation and, when the conversation needs a person, move into a human handover. The image upload work covers one specific moment in that process: the shopper can upload an image while handing the conversation to a human agent.</p>
<p>That sounds like a small addition. It is also a useful reminder that a handover has to carry the shopper&#39;s real context, not only the words already in the thread.</p>
<h2 id="the-handover-needs-an-input-path">The handover needs an input path</h2><p>A request for a human often comes with information the support team needs before it can help. Sometimes that information is easier to send as an image. Giving the shopper an image upload path during handover lets the shopper provide that visual input in the same process.</p>
<p>The scope here is deliberately narrow. The image goes into a conversation that is being handed to a human agent. The human agent is the recipient. This feature does not describe how Crossword interprets an image for product discovery or how an AI agent uses visual input. Those are separate product paths with different behavior.</p>
<p>That distinction matters in the product and in how we explain it. An image sent for a human conversation has a clear destination. The shopper is trying to give the support team prerequisite information. The handover process should let them provide it.</p>
<p><figure class="article-figure"><img src="/cw-draft-13ec83eda14bc2bc/media/image-upload-for-support/01-channel-images-context.png" alt="The current Channel Images design reference, shown as surrounding channel configuration and not as evidence of the handover upload screen." /><figcaption>The current Channel Images design reference, shown as surrounding channel configuration and not as evidence of the handover upload screen.</figcaption></figure></p>
<h2 id="why-we-kept-the-release-focused">Why we kept the release focused</h2><p>I like product work that removes a specific break in a real workflow. Human handover already asks a shopper to change who handles the conversation. Requiring the visual part of the request to travel through a separate path would add another step at the exact point where continuity matters.</p>
<p>Image upload keeps that input attached to the handover process. The confirmed release record says the shopper can upload an image during handover to a human agent. It does not document additional image controls, automated analysis or a merchant setup flow for this feature. We should be precise about that boundary.</p>
<p>The boundary also keeps the experience understandable. The shopper chooses an image as part of reaching support. The human agent receives the image as part of that support context. There is no need to turn that interaction into a broader claim about what the system does with every image.</p>
<h2 id="building-around-the-human-conversation">Building around the human conversation</h2><p>AI shopping assistants still need clean transitions to people. The quality of that transition depends on what survives it. A transcript is one form of context. An image can be another.</p>
<p>For Crossword, this is part of treating handover as a product surface with its own inputs. The handover is more than a status change in the conversation. It is a point where the shopper may need to provide the information a human needs to take over.</p>
<p>The feature record is concise: add image upload during the handover process to the human agent. That sentence sets the product contract we can stand behind. It says who acts, when they act and who receives the result.</p>
<p>It also prevents adjacent image work from being folded into this release. A shopper image used by an AI agent has a different purpose. Work that carries photos sent earlier in a chat into a later helpdesk handoff came afterward. Neither changes the scope described here: upload during the handover itself.</p>
<p><figure class="article-figure"><img src="/cw-draft-13ec83eda14bc2bc/media/image-upload-for-support/02-images-tab-detail.png" alt="A tighter view of the current Images tab in the channel design reference, included only to orient readers to Crossword's image-related channel surface." /><figcaption>A tighter view of the current Images tab in the channel design reference, included only to orient readers to Crossword's image-related channel surface.</figcaption></figure></p>
<h2 id="a-small-capability-with-a-clear-job">A small capability with a clear job</h2><p>The best support flows collect what the human needs without making the shopper reconstruct the request. Image upload during handover gives the shopper one more way to provide that context in the handover process.</p>
<p>We marked this work released on September 15, 2026. We have not found a September pull request or a merged implementation from that date that can verify the detailed interface. The product record confirms the capability while leaving the exact UI undocumented in the materials reviewed for this post.</p>
<p>That evidence gap is why this article stays close to the recorded scope. We can say that a shopper can upload an image during handover to a human agent. We cannot infer the control labels, upload limits, supported formats or operator presentation from the release record. Those details are left out until a matching implementation or product reference is available.</p>
<p>For the shopper, the job is simple: provide an image while moving the conversation to a person. For the support team, that image arrives as prerequisite context for the human conversation. That is the complete capability recorded for this release.</p>
