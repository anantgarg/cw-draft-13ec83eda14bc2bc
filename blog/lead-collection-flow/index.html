Lead collection inside the shopping conversation

19 October 2026Docs
Start with the detail that mattersOne form or a guided sequenceLead cards for direct requests

Crossword can now collect a shopper's name, email and phone inside the widget, while the conversation is still active. This is for merchants whose useful outcome from a conversation is a lead, not only an order.

The capture sits in the conversation. The agent can present a focused card or open a sheet. The shopper enters the requested details, and the flow returns them to the conversation. They do not have to leave the widget for a separate contact page.

How much to ask at once is a choice. The library covers one field, all details on one screen, or a few at a time. When to ask, at the start of a conversation or after a later turn, is part of how a merchant uses those cards and sheets. That is the shape of the original spec: capture the lead inside the conversation, decide when to ask and decide how much to ask in one step.

Start with the detail that matters

Sometimes an email address is enough. Sometimes a phone number is the useful next step. In other cases the brand wants a name, email and phone together.

Crossword has dedicated sheets for those cases. The email sheet asks for one email address and confirms that the detail has been saved. The phone sheet collects one mobile number and confirms that the brand will be in touch. The combined sheet places name, email and phone in one compact form, then ends with a confirmation.

Each sheet keeps the request specific. A shopper who is only asked for an email sees one field. A conversation that calls for fuller contact details can use the combined form. The fields are required within the form, and the sheet itself can be skipped, so a shopper who does not want to share details can return to the conversation.

One form or a guided sequence

There are two shapes in the library for collecting name, email and phone together. The combined version asks for all three in one step. The stepped version asks for them one at a time.

In the stepped flow, Crossword begins with the shopper's name, moves to email and then asks for a phone number. Name and email can be hidden when a workspace only wants the later steps. The sequence has back navigation where it is useful, so a shopper can correct an earlier entry.

A second stepped version in the library includes a phone check. After the phone number is entered, the flow asks for a one-time code and submits it before completing. That version is in the current design reference as a complete lead flow.

Lead cards for direct requests

The widget library also includes two lead capture cards for direct, form-shaped requests.

"Get in touch" collects a name, work email and a message. "Talk to sales" collects a name, work email, company size and a message, with an additional checkbox for agencies or resellers. Company size is offered as a set of choices. Both cards keep the submit action inside the card.

The cards give a merchant control over the words, the visual treatment and the submit route, while the fields stay those defined by the library. A merchant can adapt the title and supporting copy, adjust the presentation and connect the submit action to a supported route. The structure of what each card collects stays consistent. The agent selects a library card and fills it, in the same way it does for other library cards.

The same library is in the current design reference on both web and mobile: an email sheet, a phone sheet, combined and stepped name-email-phone flows, and the two starter cards. The product surface is the conversation: focused fields, an explicit submit action and a clear completion state. Merchants can use the capture shape that fits the interaction. Shoppers stay in the widget from the request through confirmation.

A completed lead is a valid outcome of a shopping conversation. The assistant can ask for the smallest useful detail, gather a complete contact identity on one screen or guide the shopper through a stepped flow, including the phone-check version in the design reference. The merchant picks the library card or sheet that fits the conversation. Those shapes are in the shared card library today, next to the product and order cards a merchant already chooses from.
